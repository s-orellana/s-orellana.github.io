I work with large-scale datasets of live human brains, like the UK Biobank or the ABCD Study (a US children's cohort), examining thousands of people at a time.

Social stress, physical health and brain structure

I've worked extensively on how early-life stress impacts immune and metabolic health to shape the brain in adulthood, letting us know a little bit better what role the body has in possibly precipitating the emergence of psychiatric illnesses. If you've read the famous "The Body Keeps the Score" book, my work tells you a little bit about how. On this theme I have also collaborated extensively on research examining how social environments contribute to resilience.

How does the brain grow?

The transition between childhood and adolescence involves not only the physical changes of puberty but also subtle-yet-significant changes in structure giving rise to adult-like cognition. We also know brains are surprisingly uniform across humans — even if we are all different, the basic stuff is there for most of us! How are the "instructions" for building a brain changing the way regions talk to each other? This is something I am looking into.

Data, data baby!

Getting meaningful information from thousands of brain scans requires meticulous work, software engineering and quality control. I've built strong pipelines for pre- and post-processing of magnetic and diffusion resonance images (a.k.a. MRI and DWI). You'll find them on my GitHub when my next publication is out.
